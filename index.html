01 / THE IDEA

Safety depends on
what an object is.

Geometry tells a robot where an obstacle is. Semantics tells it how much space to leave.

A person, a bathtub, and a sofa call for different safety clearances. But in an unfamiliar environment, the robot’s hazard vocabulary is incomplete—and raw vision-language predictions can be confidently wrong.

CoSMO turns visual embeddings into calibrated coverage sets. It plans against the most conservative safety distance among plausible labels, learns new hazard categories online, and adapts its calibration even when expert feedback arrives late.

Read the abstract+

Safe autonomous navigation requires not only geometric obstacle avoidance but also semantic awareness. Different object categories demand different safety clearances, and the set of relevant categories may expand as the robot explores novel environments. Vision-language models (VLMs) offer rich semantic representations for building maps of unknown scenes, yet their confidence scores are poorly calibrated, making raw predictions unreliable for safety-critical planning. Conformal prediction (CP) can provide coverage guarantees, but existing methods assume a fixed label set and stationary data, limiting their use in open-world settings. We propose Conformal Semantic Mapping for Open-World Safe Robot Navigation (CoSMO), a semantic safety mapping framework that transforms VLM embeddings into calibrated coverage sets and safety-aware obstacle dilations over a dynamically expanding hazard vocabulary, even under distribution shift. Experiments on the Matterport3D (MP3D) dataset show that CoSMO reduces semantic collisions by 61.6% relative to a geometry-only baseline, while maintaining formal coverage guarantees even under delayed expert feedback.
